docs: close the NetworkPolicy gap by decision, not by installing Calico

Policy enforcement was the last gap that looked "doable locally". It is not, and pretending
otherwise would be worse than leaving it open.

Flannel accepts NetworkPolicy objects and enforces nothing, so a file applying cleanly proves
nothing. Replacing Flannel means restarting k3s with --flannel-backend=none
--disable-network-policy and applying Calico: cluster networking restarts, every pod bounces,
and a mid-way failure leaves the local environment broken - all to prove a property only
staging and production must guarantee. Policy is an environment property (like TLS and
replica counts), not part of the application shape.

So:

  - the gap is closed by decision, not implemented
  - deploy/base/ contains NO NetworkPolicy objects. An unenforced policy file looks like a
    control and is not one.
  - the cost is stated: S1.no_internal_egress cannot be verified locally, and is marked
    unverifiable here rather than passing by default
  - section 12 records what staging must do instead: confirm the CNI enforces, author
    default-deny plus explicit allows in the staging overlay, and prove it with one connection
    that must succeed and one that must be refused

Updated in three places so the answer is the same wherever it is read: the environments doc
(new section 12 + gap table), the migration tracker (step 16 and the Flannel blocker row), and
the runbook's gap table.
